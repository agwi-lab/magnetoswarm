Model, equations and how to use the simulator

Using the simulator

  • Move a magnet or coil by dragging it; rotate it with the amber handle (hold Shift for 15° steps). Every parameter of every source is also editable in the panel, including AC drive (frequency, phase) and mechanical spin.
  • Pan by dragging empty space, zoom with the wheel or pinch. Hover anywhere to read B, |∇|B|| and the force on a reference particle.
  • The tube can be straight, curved, S-shaped or freely drawn (enable Edit control points, drag points, double-click to add one).
  • Keyboard: Space run/pause · . single step · r reset · f fit · Delete remove selected source.
  • "Speed" is the requested simulated-seconds per real second; the diagnostics show what was achieved. Smaller particles, strong fields and dense swarms need more sub-steps.

1. Applied magnetic field (exact, superposed)

The simulation plane is the mid-plane of block magnets and a plane containing the axis of cylindrical magnets, solenoids and loops. In non-magnetic media the field of all sources superposes exactly: \(\mathbf B(\mathbf x,t)=\sum_s \mathbf B_s(\mathbf x,t)\).

Block magnet (uniform magnetization \(M=B_r/\mu_0\), magnetic-charge solution; normal and tangential components):

$$H_n=\frac{\sigma}{4\pi}\sum_{\pm}\pm\,\mathrm{atan}\frac{YZ}{XR},\qquad H_t=-\frac{\sigma}{4\pi}\sum_{\pm}\pm\,\ln(Z+R),\qquad R=\sqrt{X^2+Y^2+Z^2}$$

Cylinder magnet / ideal solenoid / loop (Derby & Olbert 2010, Bulirsch's generalized complete elliptic integral \(C(k_c,p,c,s)\)):

$$B_\rho=B_0\left[\alpha_+C(k_+,1,1,-1)-\alpha_-C(k_-,1,1,-1)\right],\qquad B_z=\frac{B_0a}{a+\rho}\left[\beta_+C(k_+,\gamma^2,1,\gamma)-\beta_-C(k_-,\gamma^2,1,\gamma)\right]$$

with \(B_0=\mu_0 nI/\pi\) or \(\mu_0 M/\pi\). Coils: DC or \(I(t)=I_0\cos(2\pi f t+\varphi)\); two orthogonal pairs 90° apart give a rotating field. The Jacobian \(J_{ij}=\partial B_i/\partial x_j\) is evaluated per source by central differences (step \(10^{-4}\) of the source size, relative error ≈ \(10^{-8}\)).

2. Magnetization of the objects

$$\mathbf m=V\mathbf M(\mathbf H_{loc}),\qquad \mathbf H_{loc}=\mathbf B/\mu_0+\sum_{j}\frac{3(\mathbf m_j\cdot\hat{\mathbf r})\hat{\mathbf r}-\mathbf m_j}{4\pi r^3}$$
  • para-/diamagnetic and soft ferromagnetic spheres (demagnetization included): \(\mathbf M=\min(\chi_{\rm eff}H,\,M_s)\hat{\mathbf H}\), \(\chi_{\rm eff}=\dfrac{3(\chi_p-\chi_f)}{3+\chi_p+2\chi_f}\);
  • superparamagnetic: \(M=M_s\,\mathcal L(\mu_0M_sV_{core}H/k_BT)\);
  • hard magnets: \(|\mathbf M|=M_r\), lagging the field by \(\varphi=\arcsin(\omega_f/\omega_{so})\), step-out \(\omega_{so}=M_rVB/(8\pi\mu a^3)\), asynchronous spin \(\Omega=\omega_f-\sqrt{\omega_f^2-\omega_{so}^2}\).

3. Equation of motion of each object

$$m_{\rm eff}\dot{\mathbf v}=-\boldsymbol\Gamma_d(\mathbf v-\mathbf u)-\mathbf C\mathbf v+\mathbf J\mathbf m+\sum_j\mathbf F_{dd,ij}+(\rho_p-\rho_f)V\mathbf g+\tfrac32\rho_fV\frac{D\mathbf u}{Dt}+\mathbf F_L+\mathbf F_{ac}+\mathbf F_{c}$$
  • \(\boldsymbol\Gamma_d=6\pi\mu a\,K(a/R)\,(1+0.15Re_p^{0.687})\,[f_\parallel\hat{\mathbf t}\hat{\mathbf t}+f_\perp\hat{\mathbf n}\hat{\mathbf n}]\): Stokes drag with Schiller–Naumann, Haberman–Sayre confinement and Faxén/Brenner wall factors; \(m_{\rm eff}=\rho_pV+\tfrac12\rho_fV\) (added mass).
  • \(\mathbf C=\dfrac{2\pi\sigma a^5}{15}\mathbf J^2\): motional eddy-current damping of a conducting sphere (\(a\ll\) skin depth).
  • \(\mathbf F_{dd}=\dfrac{3\mu_0}{4\pi r^4}\left[(\mathbf m_i\!\cdot\!\hat{\mathbf r})\mathbf m_j+(\mathbf m_j\!\cdot\!\hat{\mathbf r})\mathbf m_i+(\mathbf m_i\!\cdot\!\mathbf m_j-5(\mathbf m_i\!\cdot\!\hat{\mathbf r})(\mathbf m_j\!\cdot\!\hat{\mathbf r}))\hat{\mathbf r}\right]\): chaining and swarm cohesion.
  • \(\mathbf F_{ac}=\dfrac{V}{2\mu_0}\,\mathrm{Re}\!\left[\chi(\omega)\sum_j\hat B_j\,\partial_i\hat B_j^{*}\right]\): time-averaged eddy force in AC fields, \(\chi(\omega)=-\tfrac32\left[1-3/x^2+3\cot x/x\right]\), \(x=(1+i)a/\delta\) (Landau–Lifshitz).
  • Saffman lift \(F_L=6.46\mu a^2\sqrt{|\dot\gamma|/\nu}\,(u_t-v_t)\), Brownian displacements \(\sqrt{2D\,dt}\,\xi\), \(D=k_BT/\gamma\).
  • Contacts: hard-sphere projection (Heyes–Melrose) with wet restitution \(e=e_{dry}\exp(-35/St)\) (Legendre 2006; Joseph 2001), Coulomb stick–slip at the wall (\(|F_t|\le\mu_fF_N\) ⇒ stick), wall rolling \(v=\alpha a\Omega\) for spinning hard particles.
  • Velocity update: backward Euler with the full drag tensor — exact in the overdamped limit \(\mathbf v=(\boldsymbol\Gamma_d+\mathbf C)^{-1}(\boldsymbol\Gamma_d\mathbf u+\mathbf F)\), stable for inertial beads; adaptive sub-steps resolve field scale, pair approach, AC period and pulse period.

4. Flow in the elastic tube

$$u(\xi,t)=\frac{Q_{mean}}{\pi R^2}\,\mathrm{Re}\sum_n c_n\Phi_n(\xi)e^{in\omega t},\qquad \Phi_n(\xi)=\frac{1-J_0(\Lambda_n\xi)/J_0(\Lambda_n)}{1-2J_1(\Lambda_n)/(\Lambda_nJ_0(\Lambda_n))},\quad \Lambda_n=i^{3/2}\alpha_n,\ \alpha_n=R\sqrt{n\omega/\nu}$$

Womersley's exact solution per harmonic of the prescribed flow-rate waveform (steady / sinusoidal / arterial pulse), consistent axial pressure gradient \(\partial_sp\), radial wall-motion velocity \(u_r=(r/R)\dot R\), quasi-fully-developed in slowly varying tubes. Reynolds, Womersley, Dean and Hartmann numbers are reported; conducting fluids (Ha ≳ 1) are outside the model.

Wall: Gent/neo-Hookean membrane tube law, \(\lambda_z=1\), thin wall:

$$p(\lambda)=\frac{G h_0}{R_0}\,\frac{1-\lambda^{-4}}{1-(\lambda^2+\lambda^{-2}-2)/J_m},\qquad R(s,t)=\lambda(s,t)R_0(s)$$

with \(p\) the local transmural pressure (outlet value + pulse + viscous/inertial drop along the tube). Valid for \(h_0/R_0\lesssim0.2\) and tube lengths much shorter than the pulse wavelength (Moens–Korteweg speed shown in the diagnostics). Particle-induced wall deformation is negligible at these scales and is not modelled.

5. What is not modelled

  • Two-way fluid coupling (dilute suspension assumed); hydrodynamic interactions between particles; Basset history force.
  • MHD body forces in conducting fluids; magnetic saturation of coils with iron cores (air-core coils only); field perturbation by the particles themselves on the sources.
  • Out-of-plane motion: this is the axial symmetry plane; particle crowding in the third dimension is not represented.
  • Non-spherical objects, shape-anisotropy torques, hysteresis of soft magnets.
